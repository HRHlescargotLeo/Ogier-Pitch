Idea One

Better ways in, for people who don't know our org chart.

The homepage currently routes visitors through three cards named after Ogier's business lines — Legal, Corporate and Fiduciary and Consulting. Three concepts follow, each keeping those lines intact but making the choice easier to make and the journey after the click worth taking.

The problem, briefly. The three cards sit low on the hero, share one visual weight and use internal labels. A fund manager arriving from a Google result does not know whether fund formation is "Legal" or "Corporate and Fiduciary" — and the honest answer is usually both, which is precisely Ogier's advantage and precisely what the current component hides. Every concept below is judged on two things: does it make the first click obvious, and does the second screen reward it?

Concept 1

The shelf

A single white plane lifted over the hero video, headline on the left and the three routes stacked on the right. Closest in spirit to the UBS component — and the safest to build.

  • Lifts the choice up the page. The card overlaps the hero rather than sitting under it, so the routes are visible without scrolling.
  • Hover gives a preview. Each row reveals its sub-services, so people self-select before they commit to a click.
  • The second screen stays put. Clicking swaps the card in place, keeping the hero and avoiding a page load.

Concept 2

Three doors

The three lines become full-height panels with their own character. Choose one and it opens across the whole band into a browsable menu, with the other two kept as rails so switching costs nothing.

  • Gives each line an identity. Right now they are three identical grey boxes; here they get distinct colour and weight.
  • No dead end. The unchosen routes stay on screen as rails — visitors who guessed wrong recover in one click.
  • Holds a real menu. The open state has room for services, a sector cut and a featured piece, so it can replace a mega-menu drop.

Concept 3

Start with the need, not the department

The most ambitious of the three. Visitors pick the situation they are in; Ogier answers by showing which of the three lines are involved — usually more than one — and the exact services, jurisdictions and people behind it.

  • Turns structure into a selling point. Watching two lines light up together is the "one firm" pitch, made visual.
  • Matches how people search. Nobody types "corporate and fiduciary"; they type "set up a fund in Cayman".
  • Nothing is lost. The three business lines remain underneath for people who already know what they want.
