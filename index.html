What we found

A firm with a great deal to say, and more ways to say it to the right person.

We spent time across the homepage, the Expertise structure, a sample of service pages and the News and insights archive. The craft is not in question: the writing is direct, the tone is distinctive and the publishing cadence is genuinely impressive. The opportunity lies between all of that material and the visitor trying to reach the part of it that concerns them.

Strengths to build on

  • A confident, human voice. "Focused on results, inspired by people" and "you don't pay for a learning curve" do more than most firms' entire About sections.
  • Breadth, clearly organised. The Expertise structure is complete and consistent across services, sub-services and sectors.
  • Publishing few can match. Deals, insights, guides, brochures and newsletters, tagged by service, sector, location, type, topic and author.
  • A genuinely joined-up model. Legal, Corporate and Fiduciary and Consulting work on the same structures for the same clients.

Where the roadmap can help

  • The homepage routes by business line. Three cards of equal weight, named as the firm is organised, sitting below the fold on most laptops.
  • The joined-up model is not visible. The site presents three separate routes; the strength of the offer is that clients use two of them at once.
  • The archive rewards those who already know. Seven filter dimensions across 4,392 items serves research well and discovery less well.
  • There is nowhere to ask. Search is keyword-based, so a visitor with a question must already know the vocabulary of the answer.

The ideas

Idea One

Clearer routes into our expertise

Two ways to replace the Legal / Corporate and Fiduciary / Consulting component on the homepage, each with a more useful second screen behind it.

01A prominent entry panel. Lifted over the hero, with the three routes previewing the services behind them and opening in place.
02Routes led by client need. Visitors choose the matter in hand; the site shows which teams are involved, and which jurisdictions apply.

Idea Two

A digital concierge

An assistant present throughout the site that proposes the questions worth asking about the page in view, and answers in the form the answer requires.

01It knows where you are. Opening questions change with the page, so the visitor is never asked what they want to know in the abstract.
02Every answer leads somewhere. A comparison, a set of profiles or a reading list — each closing with a named contact or a subscription.
03Clear standards. Four constraints, visible to the visitor rather than buried in a policy page.

Idea Three

News and insights that answer the question

A layer over the 4,392-item archive that responds to a question directly, and shows the Ogier articles each part of the response is drawn from.

01Ask the archive. A question field above the listing, with the jurisdiction switchable so the same question can be put to a different market.
02Always attributed. Every statement is numbered to the article it came from, returning readers into the library rather than replacing it.
03Four things it gives the firm. A record of demand, subject-level subscription, and a back catalogue that earns again.

Idea Four

An enquiry route that qualifies as it goes

A replacement for the general contact form: four questions that establish what the enquiry concerns, then a named team and a named person.

01Four questions, then a name. Service, jurisdiction, organisation and urgency — enough to route the enquiry and set an honest expectation.
02Context travels with it. The enquiry arrives with its answers attached, so the first reply can be substantive.
03Qualification without a gate. Nothing is withheld behind the questions, and a general route stays available throughout.
