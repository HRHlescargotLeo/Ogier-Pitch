Idea Two

A concierge that knows the archive better than the archive does.

Ogier publishes at a rate few firms match — 4,392 items in News and insights, filterable by service, sector, location, type, topic, year, month and author. That is a library, and libraries need a librarian. Three concepts follow, from a light contextual helper to a guided journey that ends in a lead.

Why the Lexus comparison holds. Valtech's diagnosis of the Lexus site was that it offered plenty of self-service and no guidance. Ogier's site has the same shape: a mega-menu running to several hundred links, seven filter dimensions on the insights listing, six legal systems and three business lines. A visitor who knows exactly what they want does fine. Everyone else — the first-time fund manager, the in-house counsel checking a deadline, the family office adviser comparing jurisdictions — is left to browse. Each concept below answers a different slice of that, and all three share one back end.

Concept 1

Ogier Guide — the concierge that reads over your shoulder

A quiet dock on every page that already knows where you are. It opens with three questions worth asking about this page, and answers in the shape the answer wants — a table, a shortlist of people, a reading list — never a wall of text.

  • Context beats a blank box. Nobody knows what to ask a chatbot; suggesting three good questions removes the hardest step.
  • Structured answers, like Lexus. A jurisdiction comparison renders as a table; a "who does this" question renders as people cards.
  • Always ends somewhere. Every answer closes with a next step — a person, a subscription, a meeting.

Concept 2

Ask Ogier — answers first, with the sources showing

Replace the magnifying glass with a question box. A query returns a short, cited answer built from Ogier's own published material, with the sources on the right and a jurisdiction switch that genuinely changes the answer.

  • Citations are the trust mechanism. For a law firm, an uncited answer is a liability; a cited one is a shop window for the archive.
  • It surfaces the back catalogue. Thousands of insights currently reachable only through seven filters become the substance of the answer.
  • The guardrail is part of the design. Scope, disclaimer and route-to-human are visible, not buried in a footer.

Concept 3

The Structuring Companion — a guided route to a real enquiry

Lexus's concierge ends in a configured car and a test drive. Ogier's equivalent is a jurisdiction shortlist: four questions, then a tailored summary naming the vehicle, the Ogier services across both Legal and Corporate and Fiduciary, and the team to call.

  • This is the lead engine. Valtech reported a 190% lift in lead conversion at Lexus; the mechanism was a guided journey with an obvious end.
  • It demonstrates the one-firm model. The output always spans two business lines, which is the argument the homepage struggles to make.
  • The output is shareable. A summary someone can email to their CFO does more selling than a brochure.

How they fit together. These are not three competing products — they are one capability at three levels of ambition, and they should be sequenced that way. Concept 3 first: it is deterministic, it needs no model, and it proves the commercial case with a lead number inside a quarter. Concept 1 second, scoped to a single practice area, which is exactly how Lexus began. Concept 2 last, once there is a content review workflow good enough that a partner is comfortable with a cited answer appearing under Ogier's name. All three run on the same need-to-service mapping that Idea One's route finder requires — build it once.
