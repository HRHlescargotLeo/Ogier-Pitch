Idea Two

A digital concierge, on every page.

The site holds several hundred service pages spanning three business lines, six legal systems and 13 locations. A concierge gives visitors a way to ask rather than browse — and, because it knows which page they are reading, it can propose the questions worth asking before they have framed them.

The opportunity. Visitors who know precisely what they are looking for are well served today. Those who do not — a manager establishing a first fund, in-house counsel checking a filing obligation, an adviser weighing one jurisdiction against another — must navigate a structure organised around the firm's expertise rather than around their question. A concierge closes that distance without altering the navigation, and answers in whatever form the answer requires.

The concept

An assistant that already knows where you are

A discreet panel, present throughout the site, that opens with three questions worth asking about the page in view. Answers arrive in the form that suits them — a comparison as a table, a question about people as a set of profiles, a question about developments as a reading list — and each one closes with a way to take the matter further.

  • A suggestion is easier than a blank field. Proposing three good questions removes the hardest part of using an assistant at all.
  • The format follows the question. A jurisdiction comparison is a table; a question about who leads the work is a set of profiles.
  • Every answer leads somewhere. Each closes with a named contact, a subscription or a meeting, rather than ending in the panel.

In context

The same assistant, different questions

What makes the panel useful is that its opening questions change with the page. The visitor is never asked what they want to know in the abstract.

On the homepage
Orientation

For a first-time visitor, the questions establish which part of the firm they need before they have chosen a route.

  • "What does Ogier actually do?"
  • "Which jurisdictions do you advise on?"
  • "I am establishing a fund — where do I start?"
On a service page
Depth

For a visitor already in the right area, the questions go to the decisions they are weighing on that particular service.

  • "Cayman or Luxembourg for this structure?"
  • "Who leads this work in Asia?"
  • "What has changed in the last quarter?"
On an insight
Continuation

For a reader who has arrived from search or a newsletter, the questions extend the article rather than restate it.

  • "Does this apply to a BVI entity as well?"
  • "What should we be doing before the deadline?"
  • "Who wrote this, and can I speak to them?"

Standards

What it must never do

For a law firm the constraints are as much a part of the design as the capability. These four are non-negotiable, and each is visible to the visitor rather than buried in a policy page.

Never give advice

It provides general information drawn from published material, states so plainly, and does not create a client relationship.

Never answer unsourced

Every answer is built from Ogier's own pages and insights, with those sources shown alongside it.

Never hold on to a question

Anything beyond general information is routed to a named person, with the context already captured.

Never take confidential detail

It declines client names, matter specifics and personal data, and moves the conversation to a secure route instead.

Effort
Moderate. Retrieval across existing pages, with no change to the content model.
The real work
Answer templates, tone of voice and the review process — not the underlying technology.
