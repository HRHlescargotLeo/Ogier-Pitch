Idea Four

An enquiry route that qualifies as it goes.

Most pages end with the same invitation to get in touch and the same general form. This concept replaces it with a short sequence that establishes what the enquiry concerns, then hands the visitor to a named team and a named person with the context already gathered.

The opportunity. A general form asks the visitor to describe their matter in free text, and asks the firm to work out where it belongs. Both are avoidable. Four questions — none of them onerous, none of them gating anything — establish the service, the jurisdiction, the type of organisation and the urgency. That is enough to route the enquiry to the right team, to set a realistic expectation of when someone will reply, and for the enquiry to arrive with its context attached rather than waiting to be worked out.

The concept

Four questions, then a name

The module sits where "Contact us" sits today — at the foot of service, sector and insight pages. It asks what the enquiry concerns rather than asking the visitor to write it out, and it ends by naming the person who will pick it up.

  • A person, not an inbox. Naming who will respond, and when, changes the character of the exchange before it has begun.
  • Context travels with the enquiry. Service, jurisdiction, client type and urgency arrive attached, so the first reply can be substantive.
  • Qualification without a gate. Nothing is withheld behind the questions, and a general enquiry route stays available throughout.

What it changes

Better enquiries, sooner

The gain is not only in conversion. A qualified enquiry is faster to act on, easier to allocate and considerably more pleasant to receive.

Fewer misrouted enquiries

Enquiries arrive at the team that can answer them, rather than being forwarded once or twice before anyone replies.

A named responder

The visitor sees who will reply and roughly when — a commitment the firm can hold itself to and measure against.

Conflicts checked earlier

Enough is captured up front to begin a conflicts check before the first conversation, rather than after it.

A picture of demand

Service, jurisdiction and client type on every enquiry gives business development a view of where demand is actually coming from.

Suggested phase
Phase one. The quickest of the four to demonstrate commercial value.
How the four ideas fit together. These are not four separate products. Ideas one, two and four all depend on the same mapping — client need, to service, to jurisdiction, to owner — and idea three depends on the tagging already applied to News and insights. We would suggest building the mapping once in phase one, delivering the entry panel and enquiry routing on top of it, and letting the concierge and the insights layer follow as that mapping proves itself.
